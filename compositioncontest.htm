What I Have Learned From My Father（第一名）

Have you seen Appolo driving his golden chariot across the blue expanse of the universe, radiating his glory over lives of all kinds? Does the sight of the spring breeze sweeping away the inclemency of the bitterness of winter, refreshing every breath of the earth, and rejuvenating the minds of all ever touch the chord in the deepest spot of your heart? My father is a great man. I have admire and worshipped him ever since I could think. The roughest but best description of him, I am sure, is only three words: savant, artist, patriot. Why, I even believe any terms of praise or description would degrade his supremacy. He is just like a fountain; whenever I am thirsty, I can satisfy myself by drinking from it. It is very clear now that I should attribute to his endeavor most of what I have learned in life. I learned from him first of all, the love of books. Who else could have been as rich a boy as I was? In the heaven of books. I have been following the path my father walked through, and every instruction of his helps my voyage get even smoother. I learned from him, too, the love of my country, my history, and my culture. To become a real Chinese, this is the great step. Therefore, when I am met with the clash of different cultures, (as one who learns foreign languages this is very common.) I can always find my root in the fertile land my ancestry has cultivated. Then I learned from him early in life the love of fine arts—music, painting, literature. He taught me piano. Oh, what a man of taste he is! What a wonderland he has given me in the domain of the elevation of the sense of beauty! So, I muse when I play Chopin’s Etudes or Schubert’s Impromptus; I dream when I read Chinese classics or Turgenev. How nice of him to have shown me into a field that most other people have not found? I learned from him, last but not least, how to become a valuable an – a man full of love , confidence, justice, and sincerity. And he himself is the best model. He is so well-behaved, hardworking, kind hearted, and self-decisive. He has a strong sense of responsibility and a lovely helping nature. In these aspects I do not think I can ever catch up with him, but I am trying, and I will ever to trying. He is to me a good father, to Mom a good husband, to our family the center and the lord. I am learning from his the willingness to serve society and to ask for no reward. Oh, there is too much to say completely! And, mundane though it seems, I would still like to send him here my heartiest praise and applaud. My dear father is the sun that gives me life and inspires my love and true nature; the spring breeze that sweeps away all the shadows in my heart and nourishes my quest of maturity. Whatever success I shall achieve in the future, I will first tell him, “Papa, thank you！”

What I Have Learned From My Father（第二名）之二

    Living in this curious world of ours, we learn a great deal from the many types of people we live with. But it is our parents who make the deepest impressions on us. Father, as the head of the family, the person who makes most of the important decisions comes to out minds first. My father has given me a great deal of knowledge---things I would have never received otherwise. This respected and dignified figure who brought me up-from babyhood, through childhood, to adolescence-this same person has had great impact on my life by teaching me, basically, how to live and how to enjoy life. My father is like an immense storehouse of knowledge and experience. He encourages me to make the best of what I have, to try my best to achieve my goals. He often said, “there is no such thing as luck. No one is a born loser. Anyone can succeed  as long as he has the will power to do so.” My father’s words inspired confidence. I remember one time when I was gloomy and pessimistic because I had gotten low grades on my tests. My father also knew about this and he told me, “life is one long trial. If you can not face these little obstacles, how can you conquer the challenges that lie ahead? Nobody’s perfect. I will be proud of your efforts as long as you try your best. Don’t give up!” this little admonition really shook me up and I started to tackle my challenges with zest and confidence. Everytime I had to do something, I would tell myself--- “give it your best shot!” because with my father’s love and encouragement behind me, I know I can do anything! In this turbulent world of ours, anything can happen. That is why it is so easy for anyone to stray from the right path of life. My father always felt that the basic principles of life were courage, honesty, loyalty and confidence. He lived by these principles and taught me to do the same. I have tried my best to use the knowledge I learned from my father, and though I have not succeed very well, I know that as long as I cling to these basic principles, I can walk on the path of life confidently, without any fear of being lost.

What I Have Learned From My Father（第三名）之一

None of them would deny that in the Taiwan of the 1960’s. the economy, the living standards everything was incomparable with what we have today. They would shake their heads and sigh. Times have changed. As to the younger generation, some are really spoiled. I change buses at Chung-men road every day, a place very close to the well known “His-Meng ting”. Across the street, I often see the spoiled children of a rich society youngsters dressed in weird clothes (or “clothe” may be the better word) pass by. They hang around all day long wasting their parents’ money. They go to movies, dance, nothing else. Perhaps they may be referred to as parasites of the future society. But I shall never become one of them. Because of my father’s experiences, I understand that there is such a thing as “hardship” and that the elders who love us are merely hiding it from us. I am willing to take on any that I may encounter. I am just too eager to work hard. If I am ever appreciated in this world, I owe it to my father, so let me end this piece by saying, “dad, I love you! Your son has understood, and shall never disappoint you!”

What I Have Learned From My Father（第三名）之二

    I was once a boy full of complaints and resentment toward my parents. I thought I was old enough to take care of myself and that I didn’t need them that much any more. Yet, it is my father who helped me grow out of this narrow mind of mine and learn to be responsible for every task I’d been given. As I was in junior high, my brilliancy in schoolwork widened the eyes of my teachers and everybody cast his praise on me. My teacher called my father and informed him that I stood at the bottom of my class. I intended to pretend I didn’t mind at all. Yet as my father hung up the phone, he gave me a confused expression on his face which which I will never forget. Later I devoted myself to studying and eventually passed the entrance exam. Were you to ask me what I have learned from my father , I might smile through the unforgettable look on his face, he had made me become aware of responsibility for the good of others as well as of my self. A boy, I think, never knows it. 

What I Have Learned From My Father（第三名）之三

    My father is , so to speak, a successful scholar. He devotee himself to finding truth in the world, and in turn teaches me what he has learned. Being influenced by him, I have got a better understanding of what life is and how to succeed in my life. My father never does things by halves. He often tells me that “if at first you do not succeed, try, try again”. So I realize that success and perspiration must go hand in hand. If you want to fulfill your ideals, you must stick to the last, never yielding to difficulties. My father also makes a point of valuing time; he never lets a day pass without reading some books. It is the realization that life is short, and time limited, that spurs me on to high endeavor. Besides, in his study hangs a paper which reads, "The taller the bamboo grows, the lower it bends." Reading between the lines, I have learned much from it. Father wants me to bear in mind the simplicity of true greatness and the open mind of true wisdom. I have made it my words to live by. It goes without saying that my father is an amicable as well as considerate man. Whenever I make mistakes, he soothes me and instructs me how to do next time instead of taking me quite seriously to task. In addition, my father is hospitality itself. He spares no effort to entertain whoever comes. All of these teach me how to get along with others and how to live in harmony with others. In a word, I owe what I am to my father. The words and the deeds of him have become my most precious asset in life, illuminating my pathway into the future. Being endowed with these precious treasures, I am sure I will accomplish my father's noble goal---to do good to our society.

We Need to Live in a Clean Environment（第一名）

As far back as the 16th century, a hearty band of Portugese sailors drifted on the Pacific Ocean. Off the shore of southern China, they Cheerfully found an island, the subduing Charm of which made all them cast their eyes on it. Awe-struck on seeing this delicate masterpiece God had created they could not help crying out, “Ilha Formosa”, meaning “beautiful island.” That was what Taiwan was first named. Now, four hundred years later, just let’s see what has become of our beautiful island. Population as by far increased, our country is marked by the surpassing growth of economy; living standards are high up. Everything is good, but the environment leaves much to be desired. The air we breathe is polluted by the rubbish fume; our drinking water is contaminated by the garbage water from factories; the agreeable beauty of Nature is ruined by sightseers. The once drinkable water become even too dirty to swim in. Oh, haw miserable it is to live in such an environment! Who is to blame for the “murder”, nobody can answer. Everybody should be responsible. For this is the very of ours. We must try our utmost to improve our surroundings. And this must be achieved by the government and the public as well. To make the air clear. We give thought to the automobiles and factories. The government should, in on time, enact strict laws to punish the vehicles producing harmful rubbish fume. This will also enhance with the factories. Moreover, factories here are too agglomerated; the government should try to, as it seems possible, disperse them properly. By so doing we can reduce the air pollution to the minimum. Secondly, we must prevent garbage from being thrown away into rivers, ponds, and lakes. With the water pollution going on someday we shall die with no water to drink at all. Finally there are scenery spots waiting to be re-beautified. Strange in that far too many tourists left rubbish, but few picked up, when they visited the sceneries. What is our Environment bureau for? They must exercise their authority over the tourists to punish them sternly when they are found defiling Nature. Only in these may can we cure the problems drastically, and regain perfect environment to live in. We are often told that prevention is better than cure. Indeed it is. But now that there are the problems, we can only check the potential tendency and try our best. We do so not only for ourselves, but also for our posterity. Four years ago, a girl singer cried, “don’t kill the world, don’t let her die. Do not destroy basic ground .Don’t kill the world, she’s all we have. The Bicycle and I

Whenever I see people riding bicycles on the streets in Taipei, a chill always runs up my spine. It reminds me of my own experience. And I would murmur a prayer, raying for these bicycle riders’ safety. I rode my bicycle to school everyday when I was in junior high school. I used to enjoy the ride very much. Whenever the weather was good; I used to bum a song and smile at people when I passed by; I’d wave to the old vendor who sold hot cakes; I loved the feeling of being up so early and breathing in the unpolluted fresh air---- all these things made my morning bike ride a pleasure. But when it was raining. Everything seemed to go wrong, for I had to balance myself with only one hand gripping the bar. While the other hand was holding an umbrella. And it was on a rainy day when the accident happened. And my fear of bicycle riding began. I remember it as though it were yesterday; stormy as it was that day. I had to go to school by bike. I held my umbrella with my left hand and kept control of the bicycle with my right. As I was crossing an unused railroad track, a motorcycle sped past. And accidentally hit my front tire. At once. I was knocked off. And fell to the ground. For a moment. I was unconscious and sat there starling blankly until and elderly lady saw me and screamed. Then, I felt an unbearable pain in my leg; lowering my head to look at it, I saw that the flesh had been torn apart, and I could almost see the bone! Blood was streaming out like a flood. And I fainted. I awoke finding myself in the hospital and felt the stinging pain in my leg again. Mother was sitting by my bed, and it was then when I finally cried and decided never to ride the bicycle again! Although bicycle riding is really good exercise and lots of fun, it’s far too dangerous here in Taipei. First of all, people do not obey the traffic rules and drive too fast, motorcyclists speed as fast as they can, huge buses are everywhere. Putting bicycle riders in a dangerous situation. Then of course, is the pollution. Think of all the dirty air you have to breathe in when you ride at traffic peak hours! If you’re someone who rides bicycles to work or to school, I suggest you give it up today! The accident that happened while riding my bike, is till a vivid memory to me. In the spring break this year, some of my best friends invited me to go no a “round-island” tour by bicycle. It was so tempting, that I was about to say “yes!” when suddenly, I caught sight of that big ugly soar. Across my leg, and the “night mare” came back. I quivered as I said “No, thank you” and hung up the phone. I realized. That the bicycle accident has not only left a scar on my leg; it has left a scar on my leg; it has left one in my memory. I dread even the mentioning of bicycles! I know now, that the scar in my memory will never heal.

Coping with Disappointment（應付挫折）

Life would be nothing without failures and frustrations. We should expect everything to carry on smoothly. It seems that in nine out of ten cases, things would go against our wishes. On the other hand, chronic comfort of life corrodes our will power. Only through encountering and conquering defeats can we gain longer and greater satisfaction, which enables us to face another ordeal. Therefore, we actually ought to take disappointment as a matter of course along the life journey.

Those who are brave and wise will view disappointment as challenge and training. If we care to study the cause of disappointment, and avoid falling into the same pitfall, then disappointment can be a stepping stone rather than a stumbling block. We need not feel ashamed of our failure. We may, instead, face it realistically, and accept it optimistically. With realism, we will see things in perspective. When we pragmatism optimistic, disappointment will not be so unbearable. In other words, disappointment need not be harmful, if it can be long borne in mind and serve as a reminder of our future efforts.
