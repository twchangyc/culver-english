(2)In my job, selling cosmetics by telephone, I get involved in some pretty unpredictable conversations. Recently, when I dialed a number, a male voice answered. I asked if I could speak to “the lady of the house.” Sadly, the man replied, “My wife is dying, and can't talk to anyone.”

I said how sorry I was, and he continued, “So am I. I wanted her to stay a brunette.”

註：die與 dye（染色）的現在分詞均為dying，發音也相同。

(6)

A pretty woman dressed in a heavy fur coat entered a Paris drugstore, asked for a slug for the scale and stepped up on the platform. At the sight of the figure on the dial, she gave an unhappy cry, quickly asked for another slug, whipped off her coat and weighed herself again. Still dissatisfied, she pulled off her sweater, took another slug, and climbed on the platform once more. Then she kicked off her shoes and bought a fourth slug.

As she stepped down from the scale, she saw the druggist hovering nearby, his hand full of slugs. "From here on," he said, "it's on me."

1.slug（自動販賣機的）代幣  2.whip off猛然脫去  3.hovering徘徊 4.it's on me由我付錢

(7)As an anti-hijacking measure, security officials at the airport in Chicago were inspecting all carry-on baggage, including packages and handbags. Some of the passengers boarding a flight for San Diego were grumbling about the security check. But one woman who had just been searched exclaimed happily, "That was great! They found some things I've been looking for all week!"

1.an anti-hijacking measure一種防止劫機措施 2.carry-on baggage隨身攜失的行李 3.grumbling口出怨言

(8)After a penny-pinching woman died, some of her relatives went to a seance. The man in the trance said, "An old lady wants to speak to her nieces in the audience." One niece stood up and shouted, "Don't take the message. She'll reverse the charges!"

1.penny-pinching視錢如命的 2.seance召魂會 3.trance催眠狀態 4.reverse the charges（打電話時）令收話者付費

(9)The main hall of the Hayden Planetarium was filled with youngsters as the lights dimmed and the magnificent star show began. The narrator introduced his talk to the hushed, awed audience by stating that on a clear night one can see 5000 stars in the sky. From the first row came a small voice: "One, two, three..."
The narrator, recovering gamely, said, "Well, most people take our word for it."

1.Planetarium天文館 2.gamely勇敢地 3.take one's word for it相信某人所說的話

　

(10)[April-Foolery]In Milwaukee, Mrs. Eugene Lee panicked when she opened her oven to check her holiday dinner. The intended feast for seven, a 12-pound turkey, had shrunk to the size of a softball.
Mrs. Lee was kept in a state of frenzy for about an hour before her neighbors confessed they had substituted a 16 ounce Cornish game hen for the gobbler while the Lees were at church. Mr. Lee had left the back door open for the pranksters.

1.Foolery愚行 2.panicked驚慌 3.frenzy發狂 4.game hen野雞 5.gobbler公火雞 6.prankster惡作劇者

(11)LIE CLOCK

Donald Rumsfeld died and went to heaven. As he stood in front of St. Peter at the Pearly Gates, he saw a huge wall of clocks behind him.

He asked, "What are all those clocks?"

St. Peter answered, "Those are Lie-Clocks. Everyone on Earth has a Lie-Clock. Every time you lie the hands on your clock will move." "Oh," said Rumsfeld, "Whose clock is that?""That's Mother Teresa's. The hands have never moved, indicating that she never told a lie."

"Incredible," said Rumsfeld. "And whose clock is that one?"

St. Peter responded, "That's Abraham Lincoln's clock. The hands have moved twice, telling us that Abe told only two lies in his entire life."

Where's Bush's clock?" asked Rumsfeld.

"Bush's clock is in Jesus' office. He's using it as a ceiling fan."
